Where low-code wins

  • Admin screens over one database or API.
  • Simple forms, lookups and approvals.
  • A handful of users, all inside the company.
  • A first version to prove the workflow is worth building.

Retool's free plan covers up to 5 users. Team is $10 per builder and $5 per internal user a month, billed yearly; Business is $50 and $15 (Retool pricing, October 2026). For a small back-office tool, that is hard to beat.

Five signs you've outgrown it

SignWhat it looks like
UsersThe whole floor uses it daily. The per-user bill grows with every hire.
LogicBusiness rules live in dozens of query snippets. Nobody can say what runs when.
Outside usersCustomers, drivers or subcontractors need access. That costs extra, or needs a higher plan.
ControlOn Retool, Git source control, single sign-on and fine-grained roles are Enterprise-only.
FitThe screen fights the workflow: phone use in the field, offline gaps, bulk edits, odd layouts.

Two or more of these, and the internal tool has become the system the business runs on.

Same trade as Zapier vs custom integration: no-code is the fastest way to prove a workflow, not always the best place to run it.

How to move without stopping work

  1. List. Every app: users, queries, data sources, owner.
  2. Pick one. The app with the most users or the most workarounds.
  3. Map the workflow. What staff actually do, not just what the screens show.
  4. Run both. New tool beside the old one, on the same data, until results match.
  5. Switch. Move users over. Keep the rest on low-code if it works.
