What gets automated

  1. Intake. Invoices arrive from a shared inbox, a portal or a scan.
  2. Read. Extract vendor, number, dates, lines, totals and tax from the formats tested for your workflow.
  3. Match. Against the PO, receipt or subcontract. Differences are flagged, not hidden.
  4. Check. Your rules: duplicates, retainage, missing lien waivers, wrong cost code.
  5. Approve. The AP clerk, then the project manager or budget owner.
  6. Post. Entered into the ERP field by field, with the source filed alongside.

Where people stay in the loop

StepWho decides
Low-confidence readAP clerk confirms the field.

The goal is fewer keystrokes, not fewer controls.

Worked example of a mismatched invoice

Suppose a supplier sends an invoice for more units than the receipt shows. The workflow should display the invoice and receipt together, highlight the difference and send it to the person who can resolve it. A second copy of the same invoice should be flagged as a possible duplicate.

After approval, accounting must confirm the entry was accepted. A rejected cost code stays on the review list. If a transfer times out, check for an existing entry before retrying. This is an illustrative workflow to design and test, using your records and approval rules.

What to check before you start

  • Monthly invoice volume and how many vendor formats.
  • Where POs, receipts and contracts live, and whether they're complete.
  • How the ERP accepts entries: API, import or screen only.
  • The exceptions that cost you today, written down.
