What certified payroll reporting is

From the U.S. Department of Labor's WH-347 instructions, checked October 2026:

  • Who. Contractors and subcontractors on federal or federally assisted construction contracts covered by the Davis-Bacon and Related Acts.
  • When. Payroll information every week.
  • Signed. Each payroll comes with a Statement of Compliance: the payroll is accurate and complete, and every worker was paid at least the required prevailing wage.
  • To whom. The federal agency if it is party to the contract. If not, the applicant, sponsor or owner who passes it on.

State prevailing wage jobs can have their own rules and forms. Check the contract.

What certified payroll form WH-347 asks for

WH-347 is optional. The information is not. Per worker, per week:

FieldNote
Name and identifying numberFor example, last four digits of the SSN. Full SSNs are not allowed.
Journeyworker or apprentice"J" or "RA" for registered apprentice.
Labor classificationFor the work actually done, from the job's wage determination.
Hours each dayStraight time and overtime, then the weekly total.
Hourly rates paidStraight time and overtime.
Fringe benefitsHourly credit for bona fide plans, or cash in lieu.
Gross payFor this project, and for all work that week.
Deductions and net payWhat was withheld and what the worker got.

A worker in two classifications gets a separate row for each, with the same entry number. The DOL also offers an online fillable version.

Where contractors lose time on certified payroll

  • Retyping. Paper or app timesheets keyed into payroll, then again into the report.
  • Classifications. A day split between trades has to be split on the report too.
  • Fringe. Hourly credit worked out per worker and checked against the wage determination.
  • Mixed weeks. Hours on the covered job separated from private work.
  • Fixes after the fact. A wrong rate found after payroll means a correction and a resubmission.

Check before payroll runs

Most errors on the report start in the timesheet. Catch them there:

  1. Job. Every hour tied to a job. Covered work flagged.
  2. Classification. Every covered hour has one. Splits shown.
  3. Rate. Paid rate plus fringe checked against the wage determination.
  4. Overtime. Straight and overtime hours split by day.
  5. Approve. A person accepts each suggested fix. Nothing changes silently.

Clean lines then feed the payroll system and the weekly report from the same data.
