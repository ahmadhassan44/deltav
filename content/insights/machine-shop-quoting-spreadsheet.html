The machine shop quoting template

One row per part and quantity. These columns cover most made-to-print work:

ColumnWhat goes inFormula
PartPart number, revision, quantity.Input.
MaterialStock size per part, price per unit, scrap allowance.Stock per part × price × (1 + scrap %).
SetupSetup hours per operation.Setup hours × machine rate.
RunCycle time per part, in minutes.Cycle minutes ÷ 60 × quantity × machine rate.
Secondary opsDeburr, inspection, packing.Hours × labor rate.
Outside processingAnodize, plating, heat treat.Vendor quote × (1 + handling %).
ToolingSpecial cutters, fixtures, soft jaws.One-off cost for the lot.
MarginTarget margin for this customer and job.Total cost ÷ (1 − margin %).
Unit priceWhat the customer sees.Price ÷ quantity.

Quote several quantities from the same row. Setup and tooling spread over more parts, so the unit price drops at each break.

Estimating CNC machine time

Cycle time drives most of the price. Use the best source you have, and note which one:

  1. CAM. The programmed cycle time, if the part is already programmed.
  2. History. Actual hours from a similar part you ran.
  3. Estimate. The estimator's call, feature by feature.

Add load, unload and in-process checks per part. Leaving them out is the most common miss.

Keep the quoting formulas honest

  • Rates live on one locked tab. Rows look them up; nobody types a rate.
  • One master file. Quotes are saved as copies, never edited from copies.
  • Every quote records who priced it and which hour source they used.
  • Material prices carry the date they were checked.

Where the spreadsheet breaks

  • Two estimators, two copies, two prices for the same part.
  • Quoted hours are never compared to actual hours.
  • Past jobs are hard to find, so similar parts get priced from scratch.
  • Drawings and RFQ emails live outside the sheet.
  • Won quotes get retyped into the ERP, traveler and inspection sheet.
