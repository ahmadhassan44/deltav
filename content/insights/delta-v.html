Definition

Δv is the change in an object's velocity. Δ (delta) means change; v is velocity. It is measured in metres or kilometres per second.

In spaceflight, Δv is a budget. Every manoeuvre costs some: launch, orbit change, landing, return. A vehicle's Δv, set by its engines and propellant, fixes which of those it can afford. Too little and it falls back to Earth.

The rocket equation

The Tsiolkovsky rocket equation gives a vehicle's Δv:

Δv = vₑ · ln(m₀ / m_f)

TermMeaning
vₑEffective exhaust velocity: how fast the engine throws propellant out.
m₀Mass at the start, propellant included.
m_fMass at the end, propellant spent.

More Δv comes from a faster exhaust or a higher share of propellant. The logarithm makes the second expensive, which is why rockets use stages.

Typical figures

ManoeuvreΔv
Speed of a low Earth orbitAbout 7.8 km/s
Ground to low Earth orbit, with gravity and drag lossesAbout 9.3–10 km/s
Escape velocity from Earth's surfaceAbout 11.2 km/s

Mission planners add up the Δv of every step into a Δv budget, then size the vehicle to cover it with a margin.
