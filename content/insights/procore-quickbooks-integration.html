Most contractors run more than one system

In ServiceTitan's 2026 Commercial Specialty Contractor Industry Report, only 20% of surveyed contractors had reached a single platform. 34% use two to three systems; 25% use four to six. The report says core systems "rarely talk to each other."

Procore plus QuickBooks is a common pair. The question isn't which to drop. It's what moves between them by hand.

Where the gaps show up

  • Sub and vendor invoices. PDFs arrive by email and get keyed in by hand.
  • Matching. Checking an invoice against the commitment, retainage and change orders is manual.
  • Compliance. Missing lien waivers or insurance certificates are caught late, or not at all.
  • Reporting. WIP and job cost reports get rebuilt in Excel from both systems.
  • Timing. Job costs in the field and the books don't agree on any given day.

How to close them without replacing anything

  1. Intake. Read every invoice from the shared AP inbox.
  2. Match. Pull the commitment from Procore and check lines, retainage and waivers.
  3. Approve. The AP clerk, then the project manager. Mismatches are flagged, not hidden.
  4. Post. Write the approved bill to QuickBooks and attach the source.
  5. Confirm. Check the accounting result and keep its record reference beside the source invoice.

Each system keeps ownership of its own records. The AP invoice intake prototype illustrates review screens with sample data. A production Procore or QuickBooks connection requires the access and mapping agreed for your setup.

Worked example of a failed transfer

Suppose the same supplier PDF arrives twice. The workflow should recognize the vendor and invoice reference and send the second copy for review. If the approved bill is sent but the response is lost, check whether accounting already created it before sending again. A mismatched cost code or missing purchase order goes back to the responsible person.

This is an illustrative control to design and test. Bring a sample invoice, the matching Procore record and the entry accounting expects. The workflow automation service describes how we map these handoffs and their exceptions.
